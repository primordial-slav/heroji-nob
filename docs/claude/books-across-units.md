# Books across units

Part of the project's notes: `CLAUDE.md` is the short core, loaded in every session, and this file holds one topic. It was moved here from `CLAUDE.md` as it stood on 2026-11-08; new notes on this topic go here.

**Books across units.** A book that portrays soldiers of many units (the *Zbornik narodnih heroja Jugoslavije*, Beograd 1957, in the Poreklo library) has no records of its own: its entries are added to the records of the soldiers it describes. `data-extraction/parse_zbornik_heroja.py` reads the book's heroes (the heading and the summary under it: birth, KPJ, NOB, death or "Danas: ...", the day proclaimed) from the cached OCR (`data-extraction/.cache/poreklo/zbornik-heroja-1957.json`, 23 MB, not in git) into `data-extraction/poreklo/zbornik-heroja-1957-entries.json` (1,187 heroes, IDs 9001xxxxxx). `python scripts/match_references.py zbornik-heroja-1957` matches them to records: sure is the same name with the father and one of birth year, birthplace, year of death, or birth year and birthplace, or a record that already calls him a narodni heroj and one more; a different father, birth years more than a year apart, a different year of death, or a hero alive in 1957 against a record of the fallen rule a pair out. `--write sure` (plus `--only` reviewed pairs) appends `reference` corrections (`ref_name` guards a re-parse); `apply_corrections.py` adds each entry to the record's `other_sources` with `ref_book`, rebuilt every run, before the links (which don't copy it: each unit's record has its own match). The field extractor and life events don't read these entries; the dialog shows the book's entry and names its page (the library's books are not linked: only its members can open them), and its words "narodnog heroja" give the medal. 384 records of 325 heroes so far.
The second book across units is *Narodni heroji Jugoslavije* (Beograd 1982, second edition, two volumes, A–M and N–Ž, in the same library; on the Sources page as two volumes, since the dialog finds an entry's book by its library address). Its biographies run on in three columns under bold headings the OCR garbles past reading, the contents as well, so `data-extraction/parse_narodni_heroji_1982.py` finds each heading by its place on the page (a short line with a wide gap above it and the biography indented under it) and takes the name read from the page image (`data-extraction/poreklo/narodni-heroji-1982-headings.json`, by volume, page and the OCR's text of the heading; 1,318 heroes, cities, units and foreigners left out). The entry kept is the birth sentence and the day proclaimed ("Rođen je ... … Narodnim herojem proglašen je ..."), IDs 9002xxxxxx; the parser also gives `death_year` and `survived` (the life after the war) for the matcher. A record that doesn't call him a hero and gives a birthplace sharing no place name with the book's is another man of the name (`birthplace` clash). 552 records of 436 heroes.
The third is *Trebinjci pali u borbi za slobodu 1941-1945* (SUBNOR Trebinje, 1974; Poreklo library, collection "Žrtve rata"): `data-extraction/parse_trebinjci_pali.py` reads its two lists of fighters, Trebinje's fallen (book pp. 13-96) and fighters from elsewhere killed near Trebinje (pp. 159-174), 892 entries, IDs 9003xxxxxx; its list of civilian victims (pp. 97-156) stays out. Its OCR reads the font's Л as А or д and Ћ as Б ("ГБАБИБ" = АГБАБИЋ, "сеАу" = селу): each word is read back as the spelling the site's records know best among those readings, the first entry of each letter gets back its drop cap, and a village named by its area of the municipality ("Rupni Do, Površ") gets "Trebinje" after it. The headings' font also prints Ц, Ћ and Ђ alike to the OCR (Џ, Б, В), and a drop cap misleads where the next entry starts another name: 70 names were read from the page images (`SCAN`, by scan page and the OCR's heading: "БЕНАЕРАБ" = Benderać, "ЏУЏА" = Cuca, "ЕРАВИЦА" = Žeravica), and since the readings follow the corpus, a re-run can turn a name the corpus no longer favours ("Šehović-Mrvovin"): compare names with the committed file. "Nepoznati borac", a man the book could not name, is `unnamed` and gets no record in Ostali borci. The book gives the father as a possessive ("Muharemov"), which the matcher compares with the site's genitive. `match_references.py --only` takes a reviewed pair whatever its grade (a death a month off, a village printed in another case). 234 records of 221 entries: 197 sure, 37 checked by hand.
The fourth is *Pali za lepša svitanja* (Al. Tadić, 1967; Poreklo library): mothers of narodni heroji tell their stories, each under the hero's short biography (birth, trade, KPJ, NOB, death, the day proclaimed). Its OCR is garbled, so `data-extraction/parse_pali_za_lepsa_svitanja.py` holds the 32 biographies typed from the page images (IDs 9004xxxxxx); the stories stay in the book. 10 records of 8 heroes; most of its heroes served in units not on the site.
The fifth, and the first whose scan is on the site, is Mišo Leković's *Ofanziva proleterskih brigada u leto 1942.* (znaci 00001/183, chapter 183_15.pdf → `ofanziva-1942-poginuli.pdf`): the fallen and missing of the 1., 2., 3. and 4. proleterska on the march into western Bosnia, 24 June - 30 September 1942, by brigade and battalion (book pp. 698-711). `data-extraction/parse_ofanziva_1942.py` writes `data-extraction/znaci/ofanziva-1942-poginuli-entries.json` (354 entries, IDs 9006xxxxxx) with each entry's place on the page and its box (the parser runs `entry_boxes`; a unit's run leaves `ref_book` entries alone), and `apply_references` copies them, so the dialog opens the page and highlights the entry. znaci re-typeset the book from its OCR, so the page itself reads "Milorađ", "Nedcljko", "Shno": the names are read back from the corpus. Each entry gives its brigade's unit code (`units`; 1, 39, 5, 40), and the matcher counts a record of that unit, or the same day of death, as agreement (sure: the unit and one of father, birth year, birthplace, day of death). 150 records of 143 entries: 141 sure, 9 checked by hand.
The sixth is the Karlovac archive's volume on the kotari of Gospić and Perušić (*Kotar Gospić i kotar Perušić u narodnooslobodilačkom ratu 1941-1945.*, Zbornik Historijskog arhiva u Karlovcu, knj. 20; znaci 00003/642, the lists' chapter 642_2.pdf by Zdenka Krivokuća → `hak-20-gospic-perusic.pdf`, its pp. 1-60 and 209-224): the fighters who fell (book pp. 986-1045, two columns, numbered within each village under OPĆINA and village headings) and the holders of the Partizanska spomenica 1941 (pp. 1194-1209, a line each; the entry's text starts with the list's title, which gives the record the medal). Its lists of civilian victims and of the dead of typhus stay out. `data-extraction/parse_hak_20_gospic_perusic.py` (a volume's config for `data-extraction/_karlovac_lists.py`, the reader the Karlovac volumes share), IDs 9007xxxxxx, 1,971 entries (1,297 fallen, 674 spomenica). The right column sets its numbers in the gutter (`entry_boxes.NUMBERS_IN_GUTTER`; p. 1's title crosses the gutter, `GUTTER`), and an entry starts where a number hangs left of the text ("1. LPO »Velebit«" at the text's edge runs on). A woman's married and maiden surnames ("RAŠETA r. PLEĆAŠ Laze SARA") are both tried (`also`); carons the OCR lost come back from the corpus ("BAKIC" = Bakić). Pairs taken: sure ones (266), and likely ones (31) whose village agrees and so does the place of death (not the općina's town, not the home village) or the day; a record that two entries of one list match (two men of one name) is left out, a fallen fighter never goes to a record from a list of survivors that names no death (`LIST_FATE`: Druga lička's "Živi u Kolutu"), and five pairs whose records contradict the entry were dropped by hand. 297 records.
The seventh is the volume on Korenica and Udbina (*Kotar Korenica i kotar Udbina u NOR-u i socijalističkoj izgradnji*, knj. 10; znaci 00003/489, the lists by Gojko Vezmar → `hak-10-korenica-udbina.pdf`, the whole book's PDF pp. 986-1035, book pp. 990-1039): its fallen only (no spomenica list), `parse_hak_10_korenica_udbina.py`, IDs 9008xxxxxx, 1,427 entries. Its OCR lost every caron (names and village headings come back from the corpus: "BELIC" = Belić, "Rebic" = Rebić), reads I as l or L in capitals ("ZlGlC", "OPACLC"), breaks capitals apart ("PUP A VAC", "C ANKO VIC") and fathers' names ("Pa je"), reads numbers as letters ("e.", "U.") or moves them to the text's edge ("8 KRAUS Bele ZELJKO"); the shared reader handles each. 536 records: 515 sure, 21 likely with the village and place of death.
The eighth is the village chronicles of the Poreklo library (collection 0fa57049, mostly SANU's "Hronike sela"; one `ref_book`, `seoske-hronike`, with a Sources entry per book that gave a match, `hronika-<village>`, found by its library address). `data-extraction/parse_seoske_hronike.py` reads the fighter lists of twelve books (`BOOKS`, `LISTS`: Medak, Prigrevica, Hašani, Beli Breg, Petrlaš, Prekopčelica, Brezovica i Stublica, Labukovo, Donje Vidovo, Donja Gorevnica, Podgočka naselja, Makce; 1,953 entries, IDs 9005xxxxxx) from the cached OCR (`data-extraction/.cache/poreklo/villages/`, not in git). Only lists the book itself titles as the war's fighters go in: victims of the terror, royal army men, prisoners, rear units ("pomoćne desetine", Prekopčelica's `drop_sections`), names only relatives gave, and other wars stay out; a list of fighters and victims together keeps the fighters (`MIXED`: "borac" or a partisan unit; `MARKED`: Donje Vidovo's "NOB"). Each list has its own style (`STYLES`: caps headings, Title-case run-on lines, tables, names two to a line); Donja Gorevnica's tilted scan is read by chaining words into lines (`deskew`). A survivors' list, or an entry that says "Živi u ..." or "Umro je 1981.", sets `survived`. The matcher grew four rules with it: a record's "narodni heroj" counts only for an entry of a hero book (`HERO_BOOKS`) or one whose text says so; a fallen entry against a record that says he lives is a clash; two entries that are not one man matched to one record, and an entry matched to two records of one unit where only one has its father (unless the other calls him a hero), are dropped. 312 records: 228 sure, 84 checked by hand. Lists of names alone (Prigrevica's fighters who joined in 1942 and later) give the matcher nothing to go on and were left out.
The ninth to eleventh are the other Karlovac volumes, their fallen only (none prints the spomenica's holders), each a config for `_karlovac_lists.py`: *Kotar Donji Lapac u narodnooslobodilačkom ratu 1941-1945.* (knj. 14, znaci 00003/458 → `hak-14-donji-lapac.pdf`, book pp. 1041-1094, IDs 9009xxxxxx, 1,127 entries; the names in small letters, Surname Father Given and what he was called: `names: 'title'`), *Kotar Gračac u narodnooslobodilačkom ratu 1941-1945.* (knj. 13, 00003/638 → `hak-13-gracac.pdf`, pp. 917-965, 9010xxxxxx, 939 entries; no village headings, the village in each entry; the list ends where the civilians' list starts on its last page) and *Plaščanska dolina i okolica u NOR-u 1941-1945.* (knj. 7, 00003/487 → `hak-7-plascanska-dolina.pdf`, pp. 689-701, 9011xxxxxx, 283 entries; the OCR spaces capitals out, "M A Č AK": `spaced: True`, a volume's own setting since in knj. 10 it would find an entry the committed IDs don't count). The reader drops a footnote by its smaller type, a page's tables ("Tabela 1.", between the spomenica's pages of knj. 20) up to the next entry, and the title's half in the other column. 495, 119 and 142 records; Gračac's fighters served mostly in Dalmatian units the site doesn't have.
The twelfth is *Oslobođenje Kruševca 1944. godine* (znaci 00001/292, the chapter 292_32.pdf → `krusevac-1944-poginuli.pdf`, book pp. 273-283): the fighters killed taking Kruševac in September and October 1944, in Cyrillic, numbered under each unit (4. proleterska crnogorska = 40, 3. and 6. srpska, 17. srpska = 55, the Kruševac military area command), with father, birth and death. `data-extraction/parse_krusevac_1944.py`, IDs 9012xxxxxx, 177 entries (the book's own count; a few printed given name first, "Milovan Jokić", are turned by the corpus). 24 records: 15 sure, 9 likely whose brigade and death in the Kruševac fighting agree, 3 sure ones dropped where the record contradicts the entry.
The thirteenth is Milorad Gončin's *U rovovima Srema* (znaci 00001/211, the chapter 211_1.pdf → `u-rovovima-srema-poginuli.pdf`, book pp. 230-267): the fighters killed on the Srem front by brigade and, under each, by the village where they fell, names only, run on, given name first ("ERDEVIK: Simo Tubić, Svetozar Živković, ..."), a father's initial or a duty after some. `data-extraction/parse_u_rovovima_srema.py`, IDs 9013xxxxxx, 5,353 entries, each with its words' box (`pdf_rects` when a name runs on). Each entry gives its brigade's unit (`units`), the village (`death_place`) and the years of the front (`death_years` 1944-1945), and the matcher weighs them: the place of death agrees with the record's death place or text, a record that has him fall in another year is a clash, and the unit and the place together are sure. 1,788 records: 1,514 sure, 208 with the place and the father's initial, 66 with the place alone where the pairing is unique both ways; a record two entries match is left out. The text layer sets some given names small ("živan Janković", read before as the duty of the man before him: `given_name`, a name the site knows and not a duty), ends a name at a two-letter or Cyrillic initial ("Sima Lj." / "Kostić") and sets a duty as a name ("Ilija Nikolić, Vodnik", `DUTIES`): a man split in two keeps the first number, and the second stays with no one (empty names, so the numbers after it stay put); read again on 2026-10-14, 11 more records. Its unmatched names go to unit 122 as thin records (a name, a brigade, a village). The Prva krajiška's and the Peta srpska's names (344 and 165) became references of units 123 and 127 when those came to the site (`UNITS` maps their headings; a re-parse keeps the entry IDs).
The fourteenth is Dragoljub Tmušić's *Sremski front* (Novi Sad 1987; znaci 00003/724, its pp. 55-160 → `sremski-front-poginuli.pdf`): "Spiskovi poginulih boraca po jedinicama", the fighters of the Yugoslav army killed on the Srem front, October 1944 to April 1945, by division and brigade, each list after a short history of the unit, names run on, numbered, surname first, a father's initial in the 1. proleterska's list ("1. Аћимовић М. Чедомир, 2. ..."). `data-extraction/parse_sremski_front.py`, IDs 9014xxxxxx, 12,812 entries in 53 lists, each with its words' box. The printer's signature at a sheet's foot ("5* SREMSKI FRONT", `SIGNATURE`) is page furniture: the reader once took it for the next unit's heading and lost the rest of four lists (840 names of the 1. proleterska, 4. srpska, 4. krajiška and 12. srpska), numbered since after the others (9014011973-9014012812) so that theirs stay put. The OCR reads a list's "1." and "11." as "I." and "II." (else two lists run into the ones before them), glues a comma to the next number ("Milenko,155.", ",’b9.") and garbles a few names past rules (`WORDS`, `SPELLED`, read from the page images and the units' records). Brigade »Italija«'s list (the Italians of the 1. proleterska division, 163) has a doubled text layer, the Latin names and the OCR's Cyrillic reading of them in the same place, which word extraction interleaves letter by letter ("AAćbee1tee" for Abete): `latin_layer` reads the Latin run of the char stream instead. The Red Army's lists at the end are not read. A record is the entry's man when it is of the entry's unit, of the same name, and has him fall on that front (`scripts/select_front_references.py`: the father's initial, a death in the front's months or years, a place of the front, or Gončin's entry already on it), with nothing against it and unique both ways; `match_references.py --only` takes the chosen pairs (a third column says why, `--start` gives the session's id range). 5,853 records (536 of them the lost names' and the names read again, 2026-10-14); 263 entries that two men of the name in one unit fit, and 34 records two entries fit, are left out.
The fifteenth is Brunović and Sović's *Bitka kod Oborova* (znaci 00003/660, its pp. 159-161 → `bitka-kod-oborova-poginuli.pdf`, book pp. 158-160): the 128 fighters of the 2. moslavačka brigada and the Posavski partizanski odred killed at Oborovo on 29 March 1944, numbered, a name a line in two columns, a nickname after a dash. `data-extraction/parse_bitka_kod_oborova.py`, IDs 9015xxxxxx, with the day and place of death; neither unit is on the site, so one record (Hohšteter Vojko of the 8. banijska) took an entry and the rest are in unit 122.
The sixteenth is the federal census of the war's dead, *Žrtve rata 1941-1945* (popis 1964), which the Museum of Genocide Victims publishes as one PDF per republic and province (muzejgenocida.rs/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/<Slo|Hrv|BiH|Crg|Mak|Srb|Kos|Voj>.pdf; cached in `data-extraction/.cache/zrtve_rata_1964/`). Each of the dead has a line under his village and općina: the name with the father in brackets, the birth year, the nationality, how and when he died, and where. `data-extraction/parse_zrtve_rata_1964.py` keeps only the fighters ("u NOBu", "u NORu", "kao zarobljenik prip. NOV i POJ"; civilians stay out) and writes `data-extraction/zrtve-rata/zrtve-rata-1964-<vol>-entries.json`, 180,619 entries (IDs 9020 slo, 9021 hrv, 9022 bih, 9023 crg, 9024 mak, 9025 srb, 9026 kos, 9027 voj; the census's own number in `census_id`), with the village in the text ("rođen 1888, Arbnež (Bar). The census names no unit, so the matcher weighs the father (a father printed in another case, "Mašan" against "Mašana", agrees: `same_father`), birth year, village, year and place of death; `scripts/select_census_references.py` takes the sure pairs and the likely ones where two of birth year, birthplace, year and place of death agree and the pairing is unique both ways. 23,892 records of 21,988 entries (the sure pairs and the likely ones above; the units and second books added since the first pass were matched by the same rule on 14 October 2026); the 150,665 men no record took (and no other book across units names) are Ostali borci's census parts (131-138). The entries files are in git (96 MB in eight files): the references and the parts are rebuilt from them.

**The census's municipalities, mended 2026-11-19** (a visitor: Bogorojca is in Gevgelija, not Demir Hisar). The volumes print each municipality's villages under a bold "Opština X" heading, and every page carries a running header naming a municipality. `parse_zrtve_rata_1964.py` let that header set the municipality too; since a page is read left column down, then right, the header landed at the top of the right column and relabelled everything after it, even past a new heading in the left column: 6,734 entries of all eight volumes stood under the wrong municipality (Gevgelija itself under Demir Hisar, Komiža under Virovitica, Blato under Koprivnica, Beška under Čoka). Only the headings count now. The IDs-keeping match (`numbered`) compares the village alone, not the municipality, so every entry kept its ID; the run also read 14 entries the old one had missed (new IDs after the last). The rebuild then went through 122 and 131-138 (`docs/claude/ostali-borci.md`): about 5,700 census-part records and 1,217 census references on unit records took the corrected text.
