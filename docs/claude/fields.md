# Structured fields and the record card

Part of the project's notes: `CLAUDE.md` is the short core, loaded in every session, and this file holds one topic. It was moved here from `CLAUDE.md` as it stood on 2026-11-08; new notes on this topic go here.

**Structured fields** (`birth_place`, `ethnicity`, `occupation`, `rank`, `unit_detail`, `death_type`, `death_date`, `death_place`, `party`) are read from `additional_info` by `scripts/extract_structured_fields.py` (per-book rules; death places are put in the nominative only when that form is a known place; a cause of death after the place, a generic hospital and what comes with it, and words of time are cut off first: "u Vojnoj bolnici u Šapcu od posledica ranjavanja" is Šabac; `LOCATIVE_PLACES` holds the locatives and misspellings the data had stored often enough to win the known places' vote, Manjači, Peći, Somborn, which `fix_place` puts right; a birthplace is cleaned of the bio's words its reader took along, a trade, an ethnicity, the scan's marks, by `clean_birthplace`: "Kučevo. student prava", "Hvar, ugos.", "Brzi Brod, Niš zemljoradnik", "Čortanovci, Srbin", while a place's own small words stay, "Šempeter v Sav. dolini", "Stari trg"). A list's heading that a parser sets before each entry, its title and the village or sub-unit it is printed under ("Pali borci, Zalužnica: Bolješić I. Ivan", "Jevreji Jugoslavije, učesnici NOR: ...", "Mosorski partizanski odred, II dio: ...: 1921, Krilo-Jesenice"), is never read as the bio (`Extractor.heading`, `HEADING_TITLE`): only its village is a birthplace, and one after a title only where no entry of the record says where he was born (it teaches the known places nothing); a village that opens the text ("Mogorić, općina Medak: 1925") is read as before, and lists whose heading names their area (`AREA_HEADINGS`: the Montenegrin Spomenica's municipality, Moste-Polje) give none. A colon followed by a space ends a birthplace ("u Stančićima: član KPJ" is Stančići); one inside a word is the scan's ("Golubincr:ma"); a note in the bio's place, a capitalized preposition with a title's word ("U Almanahu Brigade; nepismen.", "U NOP od 1942. kao pozadinski radnik"), is no place (`NOTE_RE`), while a locative printed so stays ("U Trnovcu, T. Korenica"). Only empty fields are filled; values set in corrections are never overwritten, so a wrong value an older rule stored has to be blanked when the rule is fixed (2026-10-21: the titles stored before this rule). A blank set by an edit ("") is read again where an entry still gives a value; one an edit marked `"keep_blank": true` empties stays so, like a review answer's or a family's (`structured.BLANKED`): for a value read from another man's words in the record's own entries, Bućan Aleksandar's brother's death in his merged bio (317500-317501, 2026-11-25). Every birthplace then goes through `tidy_birthplace` (2026-10-21): a part that names a duty, a unit or the party is no place (`NOT_PLACE_WORD`: "Borac brigade", "Zamjenik (pomoćnik) komesara brigade", "Lastovo, MC brigada"; nothing left, no birthplace), nor the bio's words run on after it (`NOT_PLACE_TAIL`: "Split borac", "Turčinovcu, Virovitica, Hrvat član Skoja"), a Yugoslav republic after it (`YU_REPUBLIC`: "SR Hrvatska", "SRBiH", "SAP Kosovo"; "SO Istok" is Istok; a foreign state keeps its name, "(Rumunija)"), a full stop after it, "s." for selo before it (the 32. division's "S." too), a woman's maiden or married name before it ("rod. Fiamengo, Komiža"), an unclosed bracket ("Varna (", "Trnjanama (verovatno, Aleksinac") and a first letter the scan set small ("črna pri Stahovici"); the villages Borci, Bila, Komesarac, Špansko and Grk stay. The extractor reads an em dash in the text as a comma (`dash_as`: nothing after a preposition, "u — Staniseljićima"; a hyphen in a town's name, "Kotor—Varoš"), so "u Maruševcu—Ivanec" is Maruševec, Ivanec, and under a heading of several villages ("Pavlovac—Vrebac—Zavode, općina Medak: 1904, Vrebac") the entry's own village wins, and where it names none the heading stands as printed ("Krš — Gradina"). The units' stored values were mended by these rules on that day (about 4,500, the 40 that corrections set mended in `corrections.json`); Ostali borci gets them on its rebuild. `apply_corrections.py` runs it automatically; run the script alone for coverage stats and samples (`--brigade N --sample 20`), or with `--apply` for brigades that had no corrections. Units whose parser sets the fields itself (`PARSER_FIELDS`: Druga proleterska; the pioneer company, whose entries end with deaths in 1993 and 1995 that are no falls in this war) are not read, except their books that print a bio all the same (`READ_IN_PARSER_UNITS`, `parser_set`: the Druga proleterska's Borci Sutjeske chapter and its Požega company's dead, read since 2026-10-24, 7,865 fields and 889 timelines; its own list, run-on names under a place and date, stays its parser's), in the field reader and the life events alike. A record linked to another unit's takes the fields it lacks from it, except `unit_detail` (that unit's battalion is not this one's) and, since 2026-10-27, `rank` (the Treća proleterska's "komandant bataljona" is no duty of his in the 13. proleterska); those fields are borrowed (see "The card's rows from all books"). A place of death is read through the words before it (2026-10-24, `place_words`, `BATTLE_RE`, `ENEMY_RE`): a battle, an attack or a crossing ("u bici na Sutjesci", "u bici za ranjenike na Neretvi", "pri napadu na Kotor Varoš", "pri prelasku preko Drave"), the enemy fought ("u borbi protiv Nijemaca i ustaša kod Garevca": everything after "protiv" used to go), the kind of place ("kod sela Ravanaca", "na planini Ozren") and what it lies near ("u području Kupresa", "ispred Otoka", read as "kod"), a region ("u istočnoj Bosni"), "na sremskom frontu", the Mačvanski odred's "u š. logoru" (Šabac), Čačak's "kod VTZ" and Valjevo's Krušik; where the site doesn't know the place, the phrase stays as printed ("kod sela Ravanaca, Rudo"). A death in the active voice counts ("strijeljali ga Nijemci u Užicu" streljan, "zaklali ga četnici" ubijen, `ACTIVE_DEATH_RE`), and "poginuo i sahranjen u Čazmi" keeps its place. Every stored place of death also loses, on each run of `apply_corrections.py`, what the bio ran on with after the place (`tidy_death_place`, `DEATH_TAIL_RE`: "Sutjeska, u sastavu Centralne bolnice", "Suvaja, Bosanska Krupa, politički delegat voda", "Vilića gumno, narodni heroj", "Knin, novembra"; the census's columns holding a unit, "21.brigada,3.korpus", are left as they are; 157 on 2026-10-25). A second round cut what still stood beside the place: a day without its year, the unit after it ("u sastavu 3. krajiške brigade", "u 2. dalmatinskoj brigadi"), "kao ranjenik", "je", a season or a month in the locative ("u zimu 1944.", "u studenom"), "iste godine", "sutradan", "posle rata", "prenijet i sahranjen", another version after "ili", the enemy's leader ("četnika DM"), and read the Slovene books' months ("padel 12. marca 1944"); a phrase naming a unit is no place ("prilikom napada II krajiške brigade ..."). A season or a month goes only as a whole word ("u Jesenju", "u Majuru" are places: "u Jesen" and "u Maju" had been cut out of them), a cause naming the enemy before the place goes ("od talijanskog bombardiranja u Pazarištu", "od neprijateljskih snaga kod Stoca"), and so does the unit he fell in when an ordinal word names it after the place or a comma sets it off ("kod Mojkovca, u Petoj crnogorskoj proleterskoj brigadi", "u Brajićima, u Lovćenskom NOP odredu": the Lovćenski odred's list), 2026-10-27; `LOCATIVE_PLACES` has the vote's "Žumberk" and "Ibra" since. 13,458 places of death, 1,955 dates and 1,852 kinds of death were filled so, 85% to 95% of the places of death the entries print (measured by the local `quality/fields_gaps.py`: what the entries print against what the fields hold). The birth reader (`extract_birth_info`) looks only before the first death verb, a whole word since 2026-10-28: a list's title "Poginuli, strijeljani i umrli borci NOR-a ..." held "umrl" inside "umrli" and hid "rođen 8. oktobra 1921" (the Lovćenski odred's men in Ostali borci); and a year of the war printed first with the death verb right after it ("20.10.1944, poginuo", "1944, poginuo") is the date of death, no birth ("1922, poginuo 1943" keeps its year). A birth year, and the birthplace after it, is read after the scans' spellings of "rođen" too ("roden", "roćen", "-ođen", "rojen", "rod. 1921", "Rođena januara 1911": `extract_birth_info`, `Extractor.life`; 516 years and 904 birthplaces, most of them Druga lička's memoir book, the 19. birčanska and the 7. krajiška), (an entry under a list's heading whose scan spells "roden" is read by the heading's rule all the same, so the heading's village stays its birthplace where the birth names none: "Borci poginuli u NOR-u, Ališići: ALIŠIĆ Salke IBRAHIM, roden, 1914"), and where the record's own text gives none, from another book's entry merged into it, a year a man could be born in (`normalize_all_json.py`: 300, 71 of them where an older correction had cleared a wrong one, 274412-274482); the record's own year must be one too, 1860-1935 (2026-10-28: the Lovćenski odred's OCR reads "рођен 1915" as 1515 or 1977, and the year then comes from his Spomenica or census entry). A duty is read with its unit's number between ("Komandant 2. bataljona", "komesar 3. čete", stored as "komandant bataljona": `_UNIT_NO`), "komandir odelenja", "načelnik veze", "delegat prvog voda" (1,084); a trade where the bio opens with a unit and a full date, counted from the birth year (the 11. dalmatinska's "borac 3. č. 3. bat., r. 18. 12. 1919, Ljubitovica, Trogir, zemljoradnik", the Mosor odred: 1,639; the bio's first words are read first, since that first bare year may be a later date's: "Dvorjanci, općina Duga Resa: 1927, seljak, Hrvat. Stupio u NOV 15. 9. 1944" lost its trade so, 2,899 of the Karlovac lists and Sanski Most in Ostali borci's rebuild, mended 2026-10-27), a trade before the words that run on after it ("avio-mehaničar rođen 19. 1. 1917", "opančarski radnik u Belom Polju", "službenik iz Splita": `trade_of`; never "Oficir u penziji" or "oficir u NOV"), a craft's worker or a compound the trades seen so far lack ("krznarski radnik", "avio-mehaničar"), and a duty joined to "borac" ("borac-kurir", "Borac-telefonista"; "borac-ranjenik" is borac) or the Mosor odred's "politkomesar": 4,490 trades, most of them *Jevreji Jugoslavije*'s in Ostali borci, and 670 duties. A day of the war printed right before the death verb is the date of death where none follows the verb (2026-10-28, `PRE_DATE_RE`, `before_verb_date`, from the 05 session's list): "1. 4. 1943. nestao sa položaja", "1927, Vinkovci, 4. 4. 1945, Umro", "Bil je ujet in 17. decembra 1944 ustreljen", "Odvedena u Banjuluku i 10. avgusta 1942. streljana"; never a date of another event in its stretch back to the comma, semicolon or date before it ("u NOB od 9. 9. 1943. Poginuo", "uhapsile ga ustaše 5. 7. 1941, ubijen"), a wound, arrest or capture in its sentence unless the date opens a clause of its own ("i", "a", "gde je", "kada je": "Ranjen kod Suvog Dola, kod Blaca, 16. juna 1944" is the wound's day), a span ("između 11. XII 1944. i 8. I 1945.") or a death after the war ("umro posle rata"); about 95 dates. The known places' vote (`Extractor._known`) turns a place in a case into the commonest nominative its rules give; it lacked the endings of a neuter in -e after a soft consonant, of a dropped a and of Varoš, so a genitive the records had stored won ("u Užicu" was Užica, 87 against Užice's 580). Since 2026-10-28 `_STRONG_RULES` give them ("u Čačku" Čačak, "u Ripnju" Ripanj, "kod Kaknja" Kakanj, "na Tjentištu" Tjentište, "u Metku" Medak, "kod Nove Varoši" Nova Varoš), a reading that beats one of the old rules only at twice its count ("u Križu" stays Križ), and `_WEAK_RULES` (-u to -e after other consonants, -ima to -a) only where nothing else reads and the place is counted five times ("u Velikom Laolu" Veliko Laole, "u Pljevljima" Pljevlja; "u Dunavu" stays Dunav, "u Karlovcima" Karlovci); not -ći to -ć, since Peći is a village's own name (near Ključ) as are Vranići and the other plurals; a form `case_forms.json` reviewed keeps its reading (`kept`, and `forms`: "u Brocu" Brodac). The units' stored places that the old vote had read so were read again that day (357: 290 places of death, 67 birthplaces; Kladnje, Tešnja, Metak, "na Javorku", "na Kapku", "u Brodskoj Varoši"), only where the stored value was exactly the old reading, no correction set it and no link lent it, and where GeoNames resolved the stored place to one village it was kept unless the new reading is the same place (Pučišća, Rujišta of Gračac, Ostružnja, Pčelić), with Medak, Makovište, Radeče and Radovče read by hand. A part in the war is no trade ("Smederevo, učesnik u NOB od 1943": `PARTICIPANT_RE`, 2026-10-27; the 54 "učesnik" stored before were taken off, and the reader's rules of that day gave the units 179 trades and 5 places of death more). `party` (2026-10-25) is the soldier's membership of SKOJ and the party, with the year each began where the entries say ("SKOJ 1941, KPJ 1944"; KPH and KPS as printed): "član KPJ od 1941", "čl. SKOJ-a", "član MK KPJ", "član Partije", "primljen u KPJ 1942", "skojevac", a party cell's secretary, and a second one joined on ("član SKOJ-a od 1941, a KPJ od 1944"), read from every entry of the record together (`read_party`); the year only from the same clause (the 32. division's "čl. KPJ, NOV 1942" gives none), never a candidate, one expelled, a relative's ("Brat Boško, takođe predratni član KPJ"; not "medicinska sestra" or the village Žena Glava) or a membership after the war (SKJ, a year after 1945). The record dialog and the memorial card show it under the unit as one row, "Partija" whether it holds SKOJ, the party or both ("Partija: SKOJ od 1942, KPJ od 1944", "Partija: KPJ"; the user's choice of 2026-10-26; `partyOf` in `lib/records.ts`, `t.record.fields.party` and `t.life.party`). 39,584 records have it. The Slovene books' dagger is read too: "†" with a war year is a death in the war (the parsers set those), with a later year, "po osvoboditvi", "po vojni" or alone a survivor who has died since (`umro`; the dialog then says the date is not given).

**Birthplaces** (2026-11-03, the user's "could it be that a lot of our soldiers are missing birthplaces"). Of 509,000 records 80,000 had none: about 68,000 of them because their entry prints no place (the rolls of names: the 32. division's 9,000, the Srem lists' 7,000), about 12,000 because nothing had read it. `Extractor.second_look` reads an entry the first reading found no place in, each way taken from the entries that had none: a birth whose date a comma sets off ("rođen 1925., u Brestovu", the 2. krajiška), after "r." ("r. 1927, Stenjak, Teslić"), in the Slovene "v", near a place ("rođen 1923. kod T. Korenice", stored "kod ..."), in brackets ("(1920, Dušanovac, Negotin)", the 14. srpska), "rodom od", a list's "iz X" first in the entry or after the name and the trade with no verb before it (`FROM_VERB`; the 3. vojvođanska, the 17. srpska, *Jevreji Jugoslavije*), the duty first (`DUTY_FIRST_BIO`: "borac 3. bat., 1905, Skokuće, Prijepolje"), the Slovene rolls' address (`SLOVENE_ROLLS`: "Zabiče 26, Ilirska Bistrica"); `plausible_place` lets through only what reads as a place. "iz X" says where he came from, which these lists print in the birthplace's stead, as the ten units read so before. The 12. and 13. vojvođanska's rosters stay out (`NO_SECOND_LOOK`): their places are where the men lived after the war. `no_place` no longer counts a bracket's words ("u Gornjem Zirovcu (Dvor na Uni)", the 4. banijska) and `nominative` leaves the bracket as it is. A book across units' `born_in` lends the birthplace to the record holding its entry (`born_in_form`, `record_sources.fill_from`) and gives it to a record of Borci drugih jedinica whose texts name none (the builder's `born_in` on the record, which `read_all` takes and the reader takes off): put in the nominative only where the entry prints a preposition before it, since a heading's or a column's village is printed in the nominative ("Žitorađe", "Dolovi"). What no rule reads, a case or a scan's slip, is `BIRTH_READ` in `standardize_fields.py`, each value read, for birthplaces only and by the whole value, so a form that is a place's own name elsewhere has no key there: the first table turned the Slovene Brusnice into Brusnica, Vrane by Arilje into Vrana and places of death with them, 108 values put back the same day. Check a new key against the values the files already hold. A record's own entry wins over what a link or the census lent, so about 640 birthplaces changed where they had been lent ("Beograd", where the census lists him, became his Sjenica); where his entry names the village alone and the lent value its municipality too, the fuller value is a correction (315357-). 11,900 birthplaces filled; 441,000 records have one.

**The readers' round of 2026-11-04** (the user's "what else can we fix", a list of 14). `GLUED_RE`, which stops a record's reading at another man's heading glued into it, took any word in capitals after a full stop, so "27. VII 1941", "1. KPO" and "100. KRAUS" hid at least 654 deaths: it now wants a word of three capitals or more after a word's end or a year, and no numeral. A death of a relative is taken out before the text is read (`KIN_DEATH_RE`), a death's verbs include "obješen", "ubit", "zaklao ga", "ubili su ga", and the scans' slips are read first (`scan_slips`: "1943, godine", "godnne", a month one letter off, "ubici"). `extract_birth_info` reads the rest of the births ("rođei", "roVena", "rđ.", "ro đen", "r 4. 4. 1923", a maiden name before it, a Roman month first, a bare year), the year 1800-1939. Trades after a dash, or with the nationality run on ("zemljoradnik Crnogorac"). In the census's columns `column_slips` (the offensive's name in a place, a trailing digit, 0 for o, 2 for Ž) and `UNIT_WORDS` (`unit_form`) give the unit and the place one form. Long entries were read for memoirs and notes set after them (cut, the men among them records of their own), and 144 names of the user's queue read from their pages by OCR, 1,244 kept, 2,727 left for the user: mostly a form only that man bears, printed so. "Jevrejin" is Jevrej (the user's decision; the regional pairs Talijan and Italijan, Nemac and Nijemac stay as printed, as do the 12. vojvođanska's postwar places, off the card by `NO_SECOND_LOOK`). The deaths so read brought more tails after a place, which `tidy_death_place` cuts wherever they stand ("grupacijom Draže Mihailovića", "u jurišu na most", "u toku pete ofanzive", "od pegavog tifusa", "novembra mjeseca", "u Mosorskom PO", "(verovatno ...)": `TAIL_ANYWHERE_RE`), before the place is read too, and "između", "ispred", "blizu" and "iz" keep their small letter on the card. Comparing the rebuilt files with HEAD for every field (`quality/field_diff.py --show FIELD N`) found these, and the first version's capitals, before they were committed; do it after any reader's change.

**Standardized fields** (2026-10-26, the user's decisions): `scripts/standardize_fields.py` gives the structured fields one form whatever book or reader they came from, as a list of rules (`RULES`, each a small function of a record that returns the fields it changes), which `apply_corrections.py` runs on every unit it writes, after the places step; alone it shows each rule's counts and samples (`--rule death_date --sample 30`, `--brigade N`, `--apply`). A rule changes a field's form, never what the entry says, and the entries' text is never touched; a rule that only changes a form (`FORM_ONLY`) applies to a field corrections set too. To add one, write the function and list it in `RULES`. The rules so far: `death_date` writes a date in numbers, DD.MM.YYYY ("14. februara 1941" is 14.02.1941, "15. VI 1943" 15.06.1943, "juna 1943" 06.1943, a range of days "3—8. juna 1943" 03-08.06.1943, a night "4/5. decembra 1944" 04/05.12.1944), and a date with words besides ("krajem 1942", "proljeće 1944", "početkom februara 1943") or a misprinted day ("31. 4. 1944") stays as printed (84,175 dates on that day); `captured` reads a fate "zarobljen" again in the clause after the capture ("zarobljen ... i strijeljan" is streljan, "umro u logoru" umro, "i nestao" nestao), in every entry of the man, a linked unit's too; a man let go ("posle dva meseca ... pušten kući", "razmijenjen") has no fate, and where the clause names none, a fate his entries name elsewhere wins ("zarobljen 7. mj 1944, Knin" in one book, "streljan u Kninu od četnika" in the other; the extractor had taken "ranio ga je zarobljeni ustaša" and "pobegao iz nemačkog zarobljeništva" for the man's own capture); captured alone, the dialog says "Zarobljen", and the life line draws the capture, not a death (`NOT_DEATHS` in `extract_life_events.py`, as `lib/deathDay.ts`); three old corrections that had set "zarobljen" against the entry were mended (274540-274542); `after_war` takes the Slovene rosters' "po osvoboditvi" and "po vojni" out of `death_date` and sets `death_after_war` (1,105; the dialog says "posle rata" in the page's language, `t.life.afterWar`); `albanian` writes the census's "Šiptar" as Albanac, "Šiptarka" as Albanka (1,642; the census's text keeps its word). `unit_numbers` (2026-10-28) writes a sub-unit's number in figures, "I bataljon" 1. bataljon, "XV. divizije" 15. divizije, and the scans' small letters, "iV bataljon" 4., "Vi bataljon" 6., "i. četa" 1. (11,834 values; the comrades tree reads both forms, `relations.ts`, so "I četa" and "1. četa" are now one company). The books' ekavian and ijekavian forms stay as each prints them. The site writes the days it shows in the same numbers in every language (`dateNumbers` in `app/i18n/format.ts`: 14.02.1941, a step of the life line 14.02.), the life line reads a range of days as a period, and "Na današnji dan" leaves a range out, as before. The record dialog and the memorial card show one list of rows (`recordFacts` in `lib/recordFacts.ts`, the user's choice of 2026-10-26): Otac (where the record has the father's name, one word or two joined by a hyphen; not an initial, which the name shows already), Rođen (the day where the entry gives it), Narodnost, Zanimanje, U brigadi (the sub-unit), Dužnost, Partija, and the fate by its name ("Poginuo", "Zarobljen", "Umro: posle rata"); the dialog adds its links beside the birth, the unit and the death, and the card prints the rows without them. The card's years under the name ("1919 – 1943") count only a death, never a capture or a wound (`lifeYears`). In a division a battalion means nothing without its brigade: where the division's roll names each man's brigade with his battalion (the 25. srpska divizija's "borac 4. čete 2. bataljona 19. srpske brigade", the 53. division's "1. četa 3. bat. 14. brig."), the field reader puts it in the sub-unit, "4. četa, 2. bataljon, 19. srpska brigada", "1. bataljon, 14. brigada" (`BRIGADE_IN_UNIT`, `brigade_named` in `extract_structured_fields.py`; the user's report of 2026-10-26, "U diviziji: 1. bataljon"; 869 and 709 records), so the dialog's comrades link counts the man's own battalion, not every battalion of that number in the division; the other divisions' parsers give the brigade themselves. The reader also knows the abbreviation "batalj.".
