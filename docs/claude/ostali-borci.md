# Borci drugih jedinica (Ostali borci, 122) and the census's parts (131-138)

Part of the project's notes: `CLAUDE.md` is the short core, loaded in every session, and this file holds one topic. It was moved here from `CLAUDE.md` as it stood on 2026-11-08; new notes on this topic go here.

**Ostali borci (code 122, `website/public/ostali-borci.json`).** On the site it is called "Borci drugih jedinica" (Slovene *Borci drugih enot*, Macedonian *Борци од други единици*, English *Fighters of other units*; the user's choice of 2026-10-28), its census parts "Borci drugih jedinica – Hrvatska" and so on; its id, address, files and the name in these notes stay "ostali-borci". Every soldier of a book across units has a place on the site: the entries no record of a unit took become records of their own, made by `data-extraction/build_ostali_borci.py` (62,060 records on 2026-10-21). One man printed in several of these books is one record: entries of one name from different books that agree (the father and one of birth year, birthplace, year of death; or birth year and birthplace; two hero books on the father or the birth year) and disagree on nothing are merged, an entry with a scan on the site first, then the longest, the others in `other_sources` (plain merged entries, no `ref_book`), the father the lead's, else (none or an initial) the first entry's that gives him in full, read as its book prints him (a census father beside the lead's initial: "Zec B. Drago", Bojan); an entry of the same man as an entry a record took stays out (878). The year of death is no clash where the father, the exact birth year and the village agree, or they and the place of death with the years one apart (the books often differ there, 1941 against 1944 an OCR slip as often as not). The Spomenica crnogorskim antifašistima prints a man under each municipality he belongs to (`PRINTS_TWICE`): two of its entries of one name are one man when the father, birth year, year of death and the place of death or village agree, or when they stand under two municipalities with the birth years one apart and the father in full (not one letter off; and not where they fit two such entries of another book, one each); the census's two entries of one name never are. Where a book prints two men of one name, another book's entry joins the one it agrees with best, a fit that needs the years of death to differ last, and a hero's two hero-book entries go together. `ONE_MAN` holds pairs checked by hand that an OCR slip in the father hides ("Recov" for Redžov), or in the surname (the census's "Usović" for Ćosović Veljko, whose Spomenica entry read so too until its surnames were read by the list's order on 2026-11-03, and the record kept the census's father from an entry it no longer held), and `TWO_MEN` pairs checked to be two men that the rules would join (a narodni heroj and a list that names no father, of one name and birth year: Nikolić Živojin-Brka and Han Pijesak's Žoja). Record IDs are kept in `data-extraction/ostali_borci_ids.json` (entry ID -> record ID): a man keeps his ID however his entries change, a new man gets the next free one from 0122900001 (the first records took 0122 + the book's two digits + the entry's number), so a book may have any number of entries, but a book's entry numbers must stay put. Books that give little more than the name (Gončin's and Tmušić's Srem) merge on a unit or the place of death together with the year of death or each other (`units`, `death_place`, `death_years`; the same span of years counts, and a brigade not on the site is compared by its number and name, `unit_key`: "Trideset prva srpska udarna brigada" and "Trideset prve srpske narodnooslobodilačke brigade" are "31 srpsk"). Their texts hold no bio ("Deseta krajiška udarna brigada, Đeletovci: Dušan Ačić."), so the field extractor reads only a duty from them (`NAME_LISTS`) and the builder sets the death from the entries' fields (poginuo, the village, the day); their PDFs are in `entry_boxes.INLINE_ENTRIES`, so the records keep the parser's box around the name. The file is not `*soldiers.json` on purpose: the parsers' corpora glob that, and its OCR leftovers must not teach them spellings. Trebinjci's possessive fathers ("Mitrov", "Lučin") get their nominative from the site's given names (Mitar, Luka), the possessive books' genitives too ("Jova" is Jovo's, not a daughter's possessive), and a genitive father ("Miloša", "Franca") the nominative `convert_fathers_genitive.py` would choose for a unit's record (the units get it as corrections, 122 can't), by the book's own given names first (the Spomenica's "Mila" is Milo, Lika's Mile), then the site's (`from_genitive`; the normalizer's own dictionary leaves most as printed), and fathers a book prints in the nominative, as the census does, go to `fathers_name` and not `middle_name` (`NOMINATIVE_FATHERS`: the Tuzla memorial book); `NAME_FIXES` holds names a parser read wrong, `GIVEN_FIXES` given names an OCR slip spoiled ("Miilan", "Petair"; the text keeps them as printed), and an entry its book marks `unnamed` gets no record; one the book names by a single name, a surname or a given name alone (Romano's "Ašer (prezime nepoznato)", Oborovo's "Ivica ...", Gončin's "Pančeri"), does (the user's decision of 2026-10-14), and never joins another book's entry by that name alone. `match_references.py` never matches an entry to unit 122, and `find_source_duplicates.py --across` never links to it. **Men who go to their unit** (2026-11-20, the user's rule after Dragosavac Jovice Dušan, whose entry says "Poginuo u 2. bat. 3. brig. 6. lič. prol. DNT": he fell with the 3. lička, which is on the site). A record of 122 whose entries name a unit on the site without doubt is a record of that unit: `build_ostali_borci.py` (`move_to_units`) takes it out of 122 and writes it into the unit's file, its ID from <code>900001 (a range no unit's books use, kept by entry in `data-extraction/unit_record_ids.json` as `ostali_borci_ids.json` keeps 122's), its 122 ID in `former_ids`, its books' entries as they were (the lead entry and `other_sources`), and rewrites only that range of each unit file. Which unit an entry names is `scripts/unit_named.py`'s: every unit an entry names is read against the whole order of battle (`data-extraction/novj`: 367 brigades, 68 divisions, 512 odreds, tied to the site's units by `site_links.json` and the module's checked `SITE_LINKS_MORE`), so a name that fits two units of the war fits none; a list's heading, the next man's entry glued on and the enemy's units are left out. A brigade with its division takes the table's own "N. brigada D. divizije"; else, where the division is on the site, the division; else the brigade the books call so by its own number (`OWN_NUMBERS`: the 6. lička's 1., 2., 3. lička; the 7. banijska's 7., 8., 16. banijska; Serbia's divisions their srpske brigade: "9. brig. 23. div." is the 9. srpska); the table's own numbering is too noisy for more (a date read as a number, the 7. banijska and a Vojvodina "7. divizija" of one number), so "3. brigadi VII banijske divizije" (the 16. or the 3. banijska) stays in 122. A brigade by its number and name counts where one brigade of the war bore it ("4. sandžačke NOU brigade", "II krajiškoj brigadi", "1. hrvatske brigade" for the 1. lička); a division alone (a KNOJ division only where KNOJ is printed); an odred by its name; a bare number ("3. brigada") never. The four division pages that hold only their staff at Sutjeska (1., 2., 3. and 7. banijska; `STAFF_ONLY`) take no one (the user's choice): their brigades' men go to the brigade where it is on the site, else stay. A man of the movement who was no fighter (`role`) stays, and so does one whose entries include the unit's own book, whose parser left him out (the Mosor list's activists). A man naming two units on the site, or one on the site and another, stays. Corrections may point at moved records (they are unit records); a reference correction should not give a moved man's own entry to his own record (the builder then counts that entry taken, leaves the man's other entries out and drops his record: Vukalović Petrov Marko's Vilusi entry, reference 286194, taken off on 2026-11-22 so that it joins his own). **One man's fix in seconds** (2026-11-22, the user: "If one soldier changes, we should just change that entry ... It should be a 5 second job"). A record of 122 or a census part is made anew on every full run, so a fix is kept by entry, not as a correction: `python scripts/patch_record.py <record id> field=value ... --reason "..." --by Boris` writes `data-extraction/ostali_overrides.json` (`{"<entry id>": {"for": <record id>, "fields": {...}, "reason", "by", "date"}}`, the record's lead entry in `ostali_borci_ids.json`, a census record's own census entry: 0133006479 is 9022006479) and changes the record in place the way the chain would (the value in its one form, the places, tidy_death_place but for 131-138, standardize, the "ili" versions of `record_sources`, the record's life events, the part re-sorted when a name changes): 3-9 s. The builder applies the same override to the assembled record (`apply_overrides`, right before `records.append`, after the census's and the column books' fields, the family's `fields` and `DEATH_PLACE_READS`, before `move_to_units`, so a man who goes to his unit takes it along), drops its death keys from `from_columns`, and marks the fields in the record's `manual_fields`, which the normalizer, the field reader (`main` and `fill`) and `apply_corrections.py` (MANUAL_FIELDS, SET_FIELDS) honor: no later step reads them otherwise, and a field blanked by hand stays blank. Refused: `additional_info` (the entry stays as printed), `role` (a decision; both also decide `move_to_units`) and the builder's own fields. A family's field wins: the tool refuses to set one the family's papers set, and the builder prints such a clash and keeps the family's. An override whose entry made no record (taken by a unit's record, on_site, left out) or whose record ID changed is printed by the builder; a unit's record is fixed with an edit correction (the tool says so, and follows `moved-ids.json` to say where a moved man went). `--remove <field>` takes a field's override away; the record keeps the value until the next full run reads it anew (and `fathers_name` is blanked, since `chosen_fathers` would keep it). The tool reads the kept vocabulary whatever it was learned from (`any_kept_extractor`). Left to the next full run or build: the field checks' review file, `name-genders.json` and "zvana", the places index, the war events, the search data.
