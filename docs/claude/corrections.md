## One soldier, several books

**One soldier, several books.** A soldier printed in two of a unit's books (or two lists of one book) is one record: a `merge` moves the other book's entry into the record's `other_sources` (the name as printed there, its text, and its place on the page), fills fields the record lacks from it (after reading the record's own bio first: two edits of a record's text drop the fields read from it on every run, and the other book's would win for good; 2026-10-17, after Jurčević Ante Milan's death went to the Dalmatinci book's "krajem ožujka 1943"), and removes that record (`merge_name` guards against a re-parse giving the id to someone else). The dialog shows every book's entry and switches the PDF between them; search also finds the other book's spelling; structured fields and entry boxes are read from merged entries too. Find candidates with `python scripts/find_source_duplicates.py --brigade N` (grades `sure`, `likely`, `name`, `review` by father, birth year and place, death year and place, and fate), check them, then `--write sure,likely` (plus `--only file` of reviewed `keep_id merge_id` pairs). A list of the fallen against a list of survivors (`LIST_FATE`) never merges by itself: there a matching name, father and village is as often a cousin as the same man (Druga lička, 17. Slavonska). A man one book prints twice gets both entries. A soldier in two units' books is linked, not merged: `python scripts/find_source_duplicates.py --across --write sure` writes `link` corrections (`link_id`, `link_name`; sure needs two agreements, one of them a village or the full father's name, and no disagreement), and `apply_corrections.py` ends with a pass over every unit that gives each linked record the other units' entries (with `unit_file`, the other unit's data file) and the fields it lacks; both records stay in their units. Don't re-run a second book's parser whose name repairs learn from the other units (Druga lička's memoir list uses the survivors' reader): the units change, so names, their order and the IDs shift, and while `merge_name` stops a merge with a warning, edits by id land on whoever has the id now.

**A rename and its links.** An edit of a linked record's name (one caron is enough) makes every `link` whose `link_name` holds the old spelling fail its guard, and the link goes from both records with no other sign: the diacritics batch's first apply lost 23 that way (2026-11-26). So `apply_corrections.py --apply` lists such skipped links and writes nothing (`--allow-link-skips` writes anyway). When a correction renames a record, give the corrections whose `link_id` or `merge_id` is that record the new name, then apply over its unit and every unit linked to it.

## The identity pass

**Same soldier twice: the identity pass.** `python scripts/find_identities.py` runs the three matchers at once (merges within each unit, links across units, and every book across units' references by its own rule, `rematch_references.rematch`), takes what their rules have always taken on their own (`sure` merges and links, each book's chosen references) and queues the plausible rest, most likely first, with a sample of the pairs already made for spot checks (half of it the last batch it took), in `data-extraction/.cache/identities/queue.json`; about 4 minutes. `python scripts/review_identities.py` serves a local page (http://localhost:8765) that shows each pair side by side: the facts compared, the entry's text, the scan of each book page cut around the entry; keys S (same man), D (different men), U (not sure), Z (undo). Every answer goes at once to `identity_decisions.json` (in git) with both names and who gave it (`--by`; I answer with `--answer KEY same|different|unsure --note ... --by claude`); a pair answered is never offered again, and an answer whose names no longer fit the ids (a re-parse) is listed as stale, not applied. `--write` appends the corrections (ids after the highest below 200000, or `--start`), the answers 'same' among them, and takes off a link or reference answered 'different' (a merge answered 'different' is listed, to be undone by hand); then run `apply_corrections.py --apply` (and rebuild Ostali borci when references changed). A merge moves what pointed at the record merged away to its keeper: its references, links and earlier merges in `corrections.json`, and its portrait (`portrait-index.json`, the files, `same`). Merges are taken by group: a record that is the sure match of records in two lists, or a man in three lists, merges them all into the record of the list the unit names first, unless two of them disagree, two come from one list (or one holds an entry of the other's list already: a man printed twice, or a merge gone wrong), two hold different entries of one book across units, or two have a portrait; those go to review. The answers file began with the 25 pairs sessions had taken off by hand (`by: "history"`: links and references removed from `corrections.json` since, both records still there). `--write` also points at its keeper every link and reference made to a record that a later merge took away (a second book merged after the links were written: 56 such, most of the 4. proleterska's, which apply_corrections had skipped with a warning), unless the keeper holds another entry of that book. The first batch (2026-10-16, 160401-161152): 730 merges, mostly the second books added since their matching (the 1. crnogorski and Beogradski bataljon, the 32. division's "Borci", the 8. krajiška's and 7. crnogorska's memoirs) and names mended since, and 22 links; 117 references, 39 links and 12 merges pointed at their keeper; run again on the merged records, 2 links and 9 references more (161153-161163: a merged record knows more), so run it twice after a large batch. Merges that share a record (answers 'same' on two pairs of one man printed in three lists, or twice in each of two) are one group, all merged into the record of the list the unit names first (`group_merges`), and an answer whose two entries are in one record already is done. The user's first answers (2026-10-16): 100, of which 56 spot checks of pairs already made (all confirmed) and 44 new (161164-161207). ## Your answers, checked

Since 2026-11-08 (the user's wish, after Karpathy's "give it success criteria") `scripts/check_answers.py` checks every answer of the two review pages against the data as it stands; `apply_corrections.py` runs it last and lists what is broken, and alone it loads every unit (`--sample N`, `--strict` to exit 1). An answer holds when the data still says what it decided: a fix's values (in a form the field rules may have changed), a value kept as right, a record deleted or merged, two records answered 'same' joined (one record, linked, or the entry referenced) and two answered 'different' not. A change a later correction made on purpose is no breach; an answer not written yet is 'pending', one whose id now names another man 'stale'. It also checks the identity answers among themselves: two men answered 'different' that a chain of answers 'same' makes one (the pairs undone on 2026-11-04 left answers 'same' on the men they parted, which `find_identities.py --write` would take again), and an id that is both a record of its own and an entry merged into another (an `add` given an id an older merge had freed: 0010209001, Marković Miloje and Rančić Miloš). The first run found 17 broken of 8,652 answers; the list is in `data-extraction/.cache/answer_checks.json`.
